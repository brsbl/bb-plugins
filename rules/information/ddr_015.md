# Match hierarchy to the job: peers match, different jobs differ

Sections that are true peers share one hierarchy; sections that serve different jobs get visibly different hierarchy rather than all looking equally flat.

## Why

A consistent component system still needs hierarchy to communicate role and importance, and inconsistent treatment between peers implies a difference that does not exist.

## Prefer

- Give visually sibling sections with the same job the same hierarchy and treatment.
- Use hierarchy to distinguish primary work, supporting context, and metadata.

## Avoid

- Different weights for peer sections that users compare directly.
- Giving every section the same weight because they share a component family.

## Use when

- Adjacent sections are either true peers or serve different user jobs or importance levels.

## Do not use when

- The surface has a single section or an established hierarchy owned by a host platform.

## Evidence

- Asked visually sibling action sections to share the same hierarchy.
- Rejected flat Includes and Activity sections and asked for clearer organization.

## Check

- Are the sections true peers, or do they serve different jobs?
- Do peers share one hierarchy, and are non-peers visibly differentiated?
- Can a skimmer tell which section owns the primary job?
