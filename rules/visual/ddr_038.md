# Align to perceived geometry

When a rounded or shaped container is the visual anchor, align nearby content to the shape's perceived edge using shared geometry tokens rather than to its rectangular box or with an arbitrary pixel nudge.

## Why

The mathematical box edge and the edge people perceive can differ around curves. Deriving alignment from the component's radius or shape keeps the relationship coherent when tokens, density, or component size change.

## Prefer

- Derive the alignment from the shared radius or shape token at the relevant tangent.
- Encode the geometric relationship once so content and container change together.
- Verify the perceived edge against the rendered neighboring component.

## Avoid

- One-off one- or two-pixel nudges that compensate for a single rendering.
- Aligning to the rectangular content box when the rounded silhouette is the visible anchor.
- Copying a token value without deriving the relationship the shape requires.

## Use when

- Text, controls, or nested content must align with a rounded panel, card, or other shaped container.
- Numerically equal box insets still look visibly misaligned because of the container geometry.

## Do not use when

- The intended anchor is a true grid line, text baseline, or rectangular edge rather than the shape.
- A platform-native component owns fixed geometry that cannot follow product tokens.

## Evidence

- Rejected a one-pixel description nudge and required its inset to derive from the rounded panel's shared radius at the perceived corner edge.

## Check

- What visible shape is the actual alignment anchor?
- Is the inset derived from the shared geometry token rather than a magic pixel value?
- Does the alignment remain coherent when the radius, density, or component size changes?
- Was the perceived edge verified in the rendered interface?
