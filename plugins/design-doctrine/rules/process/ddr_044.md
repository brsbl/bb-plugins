# Prove time-based behavior over a sustained run

Anything that loops, animates, or repeats must be observed across several complete cycles; correct on the first pass is not evidence that it stays correct.

## Why

Motion and repetition fail cumulatively — drift, flashes, resets, and accumulated offsets appear only after several cycles, and a single before-and-after pair cannot show them.

## Prefer

- Run the behavior through several full cycles at each applicable size before judging it.
- Capture the state before and after the sustained run at the same dimensions, and record the cycle count and elapsed time.
- Use a recording or timestamped captures when a static pair cannot prove what happens between the endpoints.

## Avoid

- Accepting a loop because its first cycle looked right.
- Two static screenshots as proof that motion stayed correct throughout.
- Treating drift, flashing, or a reset after repeated cycles as cosmetic.

## Use when

- The change involves animation, a repeating transition, polling, or any behavior that runs over time.

## Do not use when

- The behavior is a single non-repeating state change with no motion or timing component.

## Evidence

- Visual QA guidance requires at least five complete cycles, before-and-after captures at identical dimensions, and treats post-cycle drift as a failed checkpoint.

## Check

- How many complete cycles were actually observed?
- Do the before and after captures share exact dimensions?
- Did alignment, spacing, and coverage survive repetition?
