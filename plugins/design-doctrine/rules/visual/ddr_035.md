# Let control rows wrap before they compress

When a row of labeled controls runs out of width, let it wrap onto another line; do not let the controls shrink until their labels clip.

## Why

A wrapped row stays fully readable and operable, while a compressed one silently truncates the labels that make each control identifiable.

## Prefer

- Wrap the row and keep every control at its natural label width.
- Give controls in a wrapping row an explicit non-shrinking width.

## Avoid

- Forcing a variable-length row onto one line at any width the surface can reach.
- Clipping a control's label to keep its row intact.

## Use when

- A row holds several labeled controls and its available width varies.

## Do not use when

- The label is long-form content with an explicit truncation contract that recovers the full value, such as a tooltip or detail view.
- Wrapping would break a deliberate single-line alignment, such as a table row whose columns must line up across rows.

## Evidence

- Asked that panel controls not wrap into unusable stacks or clip their text as the panel narrows.

## Check

- At the narrowest supported width, is every control label fully readable?
- Does the row wrap rather than compress its items?
- Is any label truncated without a way to recover the full text?
