# Ground novel patterns in researched convention

When a decision has no established precedent inside the product, research how comparable mature products solve the same problem, show that evidence, and adopt the prevailing convention instead of inventing a mechanism.

## Why

A familiar mechanism is easier to learn than a better-argued novel one, and first-principles design is repeatedly rejected as guessing when a well-known convention already exists.

## Prefer

- Survey several comparable products, capture what they actually do, and choose from that evidence.
- State plainly whether the resulting proposal is conventional or genuinely novel.
- Take concrete values, orderings, and thresholds from the prevailing convention rather than inventing them.

## Avoid

- Deriving a novel interaction from first principles when a category convention exists.
- Asserting what other products do without having looked.
- Keeping a speculative treatment that was never grounded.

## Use when

- A surface, interaction, timing, or ordering decision has no established sibling inside the product.
- The surface belongs to a recognizable genre other products have already solved.

## Do not use when

- A product-native precedent already exists, which the closest sibling should extend instead.
- The convention conflicts with a hard product, accessibility, or platform constraint.

## Exceptions

- **The product is deliberately departing from the category convention.** Say so explicitly and give the reason, so the departure is a decision rather than an oversight.

## Evidence

- Asked what comparable applications put in a header and required the proposal follow those patterns.
- Rejected proposed designs and asked for research into comparable applications before revising them.
- Asked whether an interaction was standard or novel, and reverted a speculative change made without that grounding.
- Asked for hover-reveal timing to match what is common in comparable applications rather than a chosen value.
- Required prior-art research with captured references before settling an error label and retry treatment.
- Said not to reinvent the wheel for a common page layout and required researched references before implementing.
- Asked whether a notification surface matched the conventions of its source system and of the wider category.

## Check

- Was comparable prior art actually examined, and is that evidence shown?
- Is the proposal identified as conventional or novel?
- If it departs from the convention, is the reason explicit?
