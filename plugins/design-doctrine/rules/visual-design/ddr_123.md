# Neutral basemap, data carries the color

In map-based data visualizations, keep the basemap a natural, conventional map palette and put saturated, meaningful color on the data layer. Colors should be eye-catching but neither neon/oversaturated nor dark and dingy.

## Why

Stylized terrain or textured basemaps muddy the read and compete with the data; neon reads cheap and dark earthy tones read dingy.

## Prefer

- natural default-style basemap in plain flat colors
- semantic color applied to the data features (e.g. age, height)
- bright but restrained saturation

## Avoid

- neon or oversaturated palettes
- dark, muddy, brown 'dingy' tones
- pixelated or textured edge effects on the basemap
- decorative basemap palettes that obscure the data story

## Use when

- designing map visualizations or data-viz videos meant to stop the scroll

## Do not use when

- the basemap itself is the data being encoded

## Evidence

- User rejected a neon palette, then two 'dingy' iterations, then a stylized terrain palette, asking for a natural map palette with color on the buildings by age.

## Check

- Is the basemap a plain, conventional map palette?
- Is the strongest color reserved for the encoded data?
- Does any color read as neon or dark/muddy?
