# Lead with the decision, in the smallest shape that carries it

Open with the outcome or the question that needs answering, then support it; and choose a visual only when it saves the reader work, not because one was requested.

## Why

A report is read to make a decision, so anything before the decision is a cost, and a table or diagram that restates one sentence adds work rather than removing it.

## Prefer

- Open with the verdict, the so-what, or the decision needed.
- Present a real decision as options with tradeoffs and impact, plus a recommendation.
- Use a short flow or table only when the reader would otherwise reconstruct a relationship, sequence, or comparison themselves.

## Avoid

- Chronology, process narration, and restating the request before the answer.
- A visual, status block, or code fence for content that is one or two sentences.
- Pasting raw matrices, logs, or serialized records into a report meant for a decision.

## Use when

- Reporting results, findings, status, or anything requiring the user's decision.

## Do not use when

- The user explicitly asked for the raw record, the full trace, or the underlying detail.

## Evidence

- Communication guidance requires opening with the so-what and framing decisions as options with tradeoffs and impact.
- Explanation guidance gates any visual on whether it materially reduces the reader's work, even when one is requested.
- Visual QA guidance requires the handoff to lead with readiness and link rather than paste the durable evidence record.

## Check

- Would the first sentence alone tell the reader the outcome and any decision needed?
- Does every visual remove work the reader would otherwise do?
- Is any raw record pasted where a link would serve?
